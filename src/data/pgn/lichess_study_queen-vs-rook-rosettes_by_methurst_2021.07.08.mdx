## Queen vs Rook, Rosettes

A **rosette** is the ring the four pieces make when the queen, the two kings and
the rook stand on adjacent squares in a circle. Eighteen chapters, each one
position, working through every ring and what to do with it.

### The three types

| Type | Ring reads | With White to move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternating | colours alternate around it | a trivial win — the rook drops to a check |
| Paired | like colours sit together | the fight is real |
| Blocked | the kings are adjacent | space on the board decides it |

Each type has two reflections, so there are six rosettes in all.

### What each side wants

- **The defender** makes a rosette at every opportunity. The strategy never
  changes: move towards the centre — or to the middle of the edge when cornered.
  Usually the rook rejoins its king with a threat; sometimes it drops back a
  square or two to cut the queen's attacking lines so the king can slip behind
  it.
- **The attacker** drives the two black pieces together and the king to the
  edge. A check is the usual way in, but with less space than Black, or against
  the edge, encircling the pair works better.

> The more room Black has, the *less* the attacker wants a rosette. With the
> black king on the edge, making one is sometimes the only way through.

Chapter 1 is the position to start from, and its note is the summary the rest of
the study elaborates.
